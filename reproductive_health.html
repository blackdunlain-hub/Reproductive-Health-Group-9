Module 01

Growing Up: Adolescent Changes

Physical, cognitive, emotional, and psychosocial development timeline.

Female Pubertal Timeline

6-8 Years Old: Adrenarche Starts (Body Odor and Pubic Hair)
8-9 Years Old: Thelarche Starts (Breast Budding)
10-11 Years Old: Growth Spurt Begins
12-13 Years Old: Menarche (First Period) & Growth Slows Down
15-16 Years Old: Pubertal Maturation Reached

Tanner Staging (Breast & Pubic Hair)

Stage Description
Stage IPrepubertal (No elevation)
Stage IIBreast bud; sparse pubic hair
Stage IIIFurther breast enlargement; darker/coarser hair
Stage IVAreola forms secondary mound above breast contour
Stage VMature adult breast and pubic hair distribution

Male Pubertal Timeline

6-9 Years Old: Adrenarche Starts
9-10 Years Old: Puberty Starts (Testicular Enlargement)
11-12 Years Old: Pubic Hair Develops & Penile Growth Starts
13-14 Years Old: Peak Height Velocity, Spermarche, Voice Deepens
17-18 Years Old: Pubertal Maturation Complete

Tanner Staging (Genital & Pubic Hair)

Stage Description
Stage IPrepubertal (Testes & penis prepubertal size)
Stage IITesticular enlargement; sparse pubic hair
Stage IIIPenis lengthens; testes enlarge further
Stage IVPenis increases in length/diameter; glans develops
Stage VAdult genitalia and adult pubic hair distribution

Emotional Changes

  • Stronger and more variable emotions.
  • Increased sensitivity to criticism & rejection.
  • Greater need for privacy and independence.
  • Developing emotional self-regulation.

Cognitive Changes

  • Abstract and hypothetical thinking.
  • Improved problem-solving & reasoning.
  • Ability to consider different perspectives.
  • Executive functions still maturing (impulse control).

Psychosocial Dynamics

  • Family: Seeking independence while valuing family support.
  • Peers: Greater emphasis on friendships and social acceptance.
  • Romantic: Exploring attraction, trust, and boundaries.
Module 02

Taking Care of Yourself: Hygiene Practices

Managing puberty body changes with proper personal, genital, and menstrual hygiene.

Personal & Skin Care

  • Body Odor: Shower daily with soap and warm water; apply antiperspirant.
  • Acne Care: Wash face twice daily with mild cleanser. Never pop pimples.
  • Hair & Scalp: Wash regularly for oil control; change pillowcases often.

Genital Hygiene

  • Plain Water Only: Rinse vulva/genitals externally using warm water.
  • Self-Cleaning: Vagina regulates its own pH naturally. Avoid douching & scents.
  • Wipe Direction: Always wipe front-to-back after toilet use.

Menstrual Hygiene

  • Product Changes: Swap pads or tampons every 4-8 hours.
  • Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly before and after changes.
  • Breathable Apparel: Wear cotton underwear and unscented products.
Module 03

Understanding Consent & Boundaries

Autonomy, active communication, F.R.I.E.S. standard, and recognizing red flags.

Boundaries

Limits about what you are comfortable with regarding physical touch, sexual activity, personal information, social media, and personal space.

Autonomy

The right to make informed decisions about your own body and health. You have the right to say YES, NO, NOT YET, or change your mind at any time.

The F.R.I.E.S. Standard for Consent

F
Freely Given

No pressure, force, or manipulation.

R
Reversible

Can be taken back at any second.

I
Informed

Both parties know what is happening.

E
Enthusiastic

Doing it because you truly want to.

S
Specific

Saying yes to one act is not another.

Real-Life Consent Scenarios

Scenario 1

The Hesitant Partner

Jordan leans in to kiss Alex during a movie. Alex leans back slightly, looks away, and mumbles, "I guess so..."

Healthy Response: Jordan pauses, respects the space, and says: "You seem unsure, and that's completely fine. Let's just enjoy the movie."
Scenario 2

Mid-Way Change of Mind

Maria and Chris agree to be intimate. Partway through, Maria feels uncomfortable and asks to stop.

Healthy Response: Chris stops immediately without anger or guilt-tripping, ensuring Maria feels supported and safe.
Scenario 3

Peer & Digital Pressure

Sam continuously asks Taylor to send private intimate photos, saying "Everyone does it, don't you trust me?"

Healthy Response: Taylor sets a firm boundary: "No, I'm not comfortable with that." Sam respects it immediately.
Module 04

Making Healthy Choices & Peer Pressure

The STOP & THINK decision-making framework and assertive communication.

S

Stop

Pause before acting. Step away from pressure or emotionally charged situations.

T

Think

What are my options? What could happen immediately or later? Is this safe?

O

Observe

Look at your safer alternatives and evaluate your personal values and boundaries.

P

Proceed

Choose the option that best protects your health, safety, and future.

Assertive Communication

Expressing your thoughts, feelings, and boundaries clearly while respecting others.

  • Say "No" clearly and confidently.
  • Use "I" statements: "I don't feel comfortable doing that."
  • Maintain calm body language and eye contact.
  • Suggest a safe alternative when possible.

Healthy Relationships & Support

Built on mutual respect, trust, communication, consent, and support.

Trusted Help Sources: Trusted parents/guardians, school counselors, teachers, or qualified healthcare professionals.
Key Message: Asking for help is a sign of responsibility and strength, never weakness.
