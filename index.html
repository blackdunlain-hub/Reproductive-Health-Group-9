Interactive Learning Modules

Physical, Emotional, & Psychosocial Changes

Physical Changes (Puberty)

  • Adrenarche & Thelarche (6–9 years)[cite: 5, 6]
  • Growth spurts & peak height velocity[cite: 5, 6]
  • Menarche in females (12–13 years)[cite: 5]
  • Spermarche & voice changes in males (13–14 years)[cite: 6]

Emotional & Psychosocial Shifts

  • Stronger, more variable emotions & privacy needs[cite: 7]
  • Identity exploration ("Who am I?")[cite: 8]
  • Shifting focus towards peers and social belonging[cite: 8]
  • Body image awareness & self-esteem development[cite: 9]

Personal, Menstrual, & Genital Hygiene

Puberty Body Management

  • Body Odor: Shower daily with soap; use antiperspirant/deodorant[cite: 10].
  • Skin Care: Wash face twice daily with a mild cleanser; never pop pimples[cite: 10].
  • Hair Care: Shampoo regularly to control scalp sebum and oil[cite: 10].

Genital & Menstrual Care

  • Genital Care: Use plain warm water externally; wipe front-to-back; avoid scents/douching[cite: 11].
  • Menstrual Hygiene: Change pads/tampons every 4-8 hours with clean hands[cite: 11].
  • Apparel: Wear breathable cotton underwear daily[cite: 11].

Understanding Consent & Boundaries

Boundaries & Autonomy

You have the right to say YES, NO, NOT YET, or change your mind at any moment[cite: 12].

The F.R.I.E.S. Standard

  • Freely given (no pressure)[cite: 14]
  • Reversible (can be withdrawn anytime)[cite: 14]
  • Informed (knowing what's happening)[cite: 14]
  • Enthusiastic (wanting to do it)[cite: 14]
  • Specific (yes to one thing isn't yes to all)[cite: 14]

Recognizing Coercion & Red Flags

  • Guilt-tripping ("If you loved me...")[cite: 15]
  • Wearing someone down through repeated pleading[cite: 15]
  • Threats, conditional affection, or intimidation[cite: 15]
  • Incapacitation due to alcohol, drugs, or sleep[cite: 15]

Decision-Making, Peer Pressure, & Support

The STOP & THINK Framework

  • Stop: Pause before acting[cite: 18, 23].
  • Think: Evaluate short & long-term consequences[cite: 18, 23].
  • Observe: Look at your safer alternatives[cite: 23].
  • Proceed: Choose options protecting health & values[cite: 23].

Assertive Communication & Support

  • Use clear "I" statements ("I don't feel comfortable...")[cite: 21].
  • Maintain calm body language and confident eye contact[cite: 21].
  • Seeking Help: Talk to trusted parents, teachers, school counselors, or health professionals[cite: 22].
